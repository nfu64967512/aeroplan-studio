abstract
Complex multi-area collaborative coverage path planning in dynamic environments poses a signi ﬁcant
challenge for multi- ﬁxed-wing UAVs (multi-UAV). This study establishes a comprehensive framework
that incorporates UAV capabilities, terrain, complex areas, and mission dynamics. A novel dynamic
collaborative path planning algorithm is introduced, designed to ensure complete coverage of designated
areas. This algorithm meticulously optimizes the operation, entry, and transition paths for each UAV,
while also establishing evaluation metrics to re ﬁne coverage sequences for each area. Additionally, a
three-dimensional path is computed utilizing an altitude descent method, effectively integrating two-
dimensional coverage paths with altitude constraints. The ef ﬁcacy of the proposed approach is vali-
dated through digital simulations and mixed-reality semi-physical experiments across a variety of dy-
namic scenarios, including both single-area and multi-area coverage by multi-UAV. Results show that the
coverage paths generated by this method signi ﬁcantly reduce both computation time and path length,
providing a reliable solution for dynamic multi-UAV mission planning in semi-physical environments.

1. Introduction
In modern military rescue and reconnaissance missions in
maritime and mountainous areas [1e3], it is essential to cover high-
threat, hard-to-access areas quickly to locate targets [ 4]. Fixed-
wing UAVs (Unmanned Aerial Vehicles), with their ﬂexibility,
convenience, and excellent aerial accessibility, have demonstrated
unique advantages in this domain [ 5]. With lower costs and ad-
vancements in UAV swarm technology, multiple UAVs working
together can enhance mission cost-effectiveness and are ideal for
real-time, complete area coverage [ 6,7].
The main goal of such missions is to quickly and ef ﬁciently cover
designated areas. Multi-UAV can autonomously navigate along pre-
planned paths, adjusting dynamically in response to real-time
changes. To handle mission constraints, Dynamic Complete
Coverage Path Planning (DCCPP) minimizes ﬂight distance while
ensuring complete coverage and collision avoidance, enhancing
UAV survivability and ef ﬁciency [8,9].
DCCPP assigns multi-UAV to cover de ﬁned areas by creating a
network of nodes and arcs in a 3D environment. This results in a
typical multi-constraint multi-depot vehicle routing problem
(MDVRP) [ 10,11]. The MDVRP is a combinatorial optimization
problem, formulated using a mixed-integer programming (MIP)
model, where determining optimal solutions is NP-hard [ 12]. The
process considers factors such as UAV ﬁeld of view (FOV), coverage
overlap, coverage direction, path length, the number of turns,
turning radius, and terrain altitude [ 13,14]. Traditional path plan-
ning focuses on point-to-point problems, where differential motion
equations are established based on control variables such as ac-
celeration or load, and optimal control strategies are employed to
determine a path from the starting point to the endpoint [ 15].
Major solution methods include the pseudospectral method [16,17],
the arti ﬁcial potential ﬁeld method [ 18,19], the improved arti ﬁcial
electric ﬁeld algorithm [ 20], and the model predictive control
method [ 21,22]. However, these methods are only applicable to
small-scale problems and are inadequate for the demands of
coverage-oriented missions.
Currently, many scholars have conducted research on path
planning for area coverage missions. Two primary modeling and
solution approaches have been established based on constraints
such as UAV turning angle and turning radius. The ﬁrst approach
employs grid-based methods that discretize the area into grids
according to the UAV's FOV capability, solving for the optimal
coverage path using heuristic graph search techniques [ 23e26].
Modares et al. [ 27] considered the UAV's energy consumption
characteristics and applied a modi ﬁed Lin-Kernighan heuristic

problem. Motamedi et al. [ 28] introduced an ant colony optimiza-
tion algorithm to address various constraints in dynamic environ-
ments, while Dubins curves were utilized to account for the
kinematic limitations of ﬁxed-wing UAVs when planning tangent-
continuous paths. Cho et al. [ 29] aimed to minimize mission
completion time by modeling the graph search process of multi-
UAV CPP as a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model. A
randomized search heuristic (RSH) algorithm was developed, and
its effectiveness was validated through experiments. However, as
the area expands, the number of grids increases exponentially,
leading to longer computation times, making these methods un-
suitable for real-time complete coverage path planning in emer-
gency situations. Grid division based on FOV characteristics can
cause path inef ﬁciencies when transitioning between grids, espe-
cially for ﬁxed-wing UAVs with large turning radius. This limitation
signiﬁcantly reduces the effective coverage area of a single UAV.
Therefore, these methods are more suitable for area coverage
missions involving rotary-wing UAVs or ﬁxed-wing UAVs with
smaller turning radius.
The second approach to solving DCCPP problems adopts a "path-
ﬁrst, sequence-second" strategy. This involves dividing the area
into sub-areas of various shapes according to the UAVs' FOV capa-
bilities, with each sub-area corresponding to a different operational
path, referred to as a cell. The problem is then formulated as a
multi-traveling salesman problem (MTSP), where the objective is to
optimize the conﬁguration of multi-UAV cell nodes to minimize the
total path distance [ 30]. For example, Chen et al. [ 31] proposed an
area division algorithm based on the area width and UAV FOV
capability, which minimizes the total number of UAV turns. Build-
ing on this, Cao et al. [ 32] addressed the issues of high repetition
rates and excessive turns by introducing a turn constraint and a
back-and-forth pattern, which greatly enhanced path planning ef-
ﬁciency. B€ahnemann et al. [ 33] introduced an exact cell decompo-
sition method, solving the generalized traveling salesman problem
on the cell adjacency graph to minimize path time. Furthermore,
enumeration schemes [ 34] and evolutionary computation algo-
rithms [35], such as genetic algorithms [ 36], have also been applied
to optimize the DCCPP problem. Mayilvaganam et al. [ 37] used the
ant colony optimization algorithm to further improve the perfor-
mance of the line-sweep-based decomposition method to ﬁnd the
polygon visiting order that generates minimum path length.
Compared to the ﬁrst approach, the second approach is more
advantageous for solving the DCCPP problem involving multi-UAV
with stricter maneuverability constraints. However, existing
studies have not fully considered the dynamic costs associated with
different path transition directions during sequence optimization.
Moreover, most of these studies rely on heuristic methods. Due to
the in ﬂuence of random factors inherent in these methods, the
results often exhibit poor convergence and high randomness,
posing signiﬁcant challenges to practical applications. Additionally,
due to their strong risk tolerance, multi-UAV can closely simulate
real-world scenarios by performing complete, partial, and group-
level adjustments, which enables them to respond effectively to
unexpected mission events [ 38]. To address these limitations and
ensure high reliability, our study contributes in the following ways:
/C15 Efﬁcient area-based decomposition and path planning: We
introduce an ef ﬁcient method for area-based decomposition
and path planning for rapid coverage across multi-area. Tradi-
tional CPP approaches often neglect path length wasted during
transitions, compromising optimality in large and complex
areas. Our study addresses this by developing a robust path
optimization model.
/C15 Optimized multi-UAV allocation and area coverage: We propose
strategies for generating operation paths and allocating areas
that consider constraints like area shape, UAV count, and FOV
capabilities, reducing the lower bound of mission completion
time.
/C15 Improved dynamic programming for DCCPP: To avoid local op-
tima and slow optimization speeds in heuristic algorithms, we
improve the dynamic programming algorithm to effectively
solve the DCCPP problem under dynamic conditions. Our study
demonstrates superior performance through extensive numer-
ical and semi-physical simulations, offering insights for practical
applications in real-world environments.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2
provides a detailed problem description and modeling. In Section
3, we describe the proposed algorithm, followed by computational
experiments and their interpretation in Section 4. Finally, conclu-
sions are drawn in Section 5.
2. Modeling of the multi-UAV DCCPP problem
When conducting a collaborative mission, a multi-UAV system
must navigate a complex mission area containing multiple poten-
tial targets. As ﬁxed-wing UAVs follow their planned paths, the
dynamic environment may introduce new areas requiring coverage
at any moment. The primary goal is to achieve complete coverage of
these emerging areas while minimizing overall mission time.
To address this challenge, we divide the problem model into two
phases. In Phase 1 of Section 2.2, we describe the UAV capabilities,
the environment terrain, and the complex area decomposition
models, which are used to initialize the problem. In Phase 2 of
Section 2.3, we present the DCCPP mission and path simulation
model, aiming to minimize the mission completion time. Mathe-
matical notations and de ﬁnitions for this section are listed in
Table 1.
2.1. Assumption
To formulate a mathematical model, the following assumptions
are introduced:
/C15 All ﬁxed-wing UAVs are homogeneous and continuously update
environmental knowledge based on their observations and
shared data.
/C15 Each UAV starts from an initial position outside the designated
multi-area coverage zones and maintains a constant speed.
/C15 The ground projection shape of the FOV for all UAVs is identical,
as shown in Fig. 1., and the FOV remains constant during ascent
or descent.
/C15 During the ascent and descent phases, the UAV maintains a
constant ground speed.
2.2. Phase 1: Initialization modeling, and area decomposition
To propose an effective method for multi-UAV operations with
strict kinematic constraints in the presence of both static and dy-
namic missions, this phase outlines the UAV model, mission envi-
ronment, and complex area models for initializing the problem.

2.2.2. Modeling of environmental terrain
When multi-UAV execute coverage mission in 3D space, they
must continuously retrieve accurate positional data from the
original terrain model [ 20]. Flying at a constant altitude limits their
effectiveness in covering complex terrains, such as mountainous
and valley areas, as safety restrictions prevent them from navi-
gating closer to the Earth's surface. Additionally, ﬂying too far from
the surface can result in an unclear FOV. Therefore, in the path
planning process for Ui, it is essential to maintain speci ﬁc relative
altitude constraints hi2½hmin; hmax/C138 with respect to the local terrain.
Each UAV's altitude is optimized based on its climbing ability,
sensor capacity, and the terrain characteristics of the area, adjusting
its 3D ﬂight path accordingly.

2.2.3. Modeling of complex area
The boundary of a complex area in natural terrain often forms a
random contour with multiple concave edges. To facilitate the
study of the DCCPP algorithm, this irregular area is approximated as
a corresponding complex polygon, and the Douglas-Peucker algo-
rithm is applied to simplify and convexify area Zk [42], as shown in
Fig. 3 (a).
Once simpli ﬁed, area Zk is divided into parallel lines extending
from the edge Ek;mðmþ1Þ to its furthest points (or edge), as shown in
Figs. 3 (b) and 3(c). Research indicates that the direction perpen-
dicular to the minimum width wk;min of the polygon and parallel to
one of its sides minimizes the number of turns required during
multi-UAV coverage of the convex polygon area [ 43]. To determine
this minimum width, calculate the distance dis k;mu between
Ek;mðmþ1Þ and each point vk;u of Zk. Then, the minimum width
wk;min ¼ minðdisk;muÞ of Zk can be obtained, with the corresponding
edge and its furthest vertex denoted as Ek;min and vk;min, respec-

angle for covering Zk corresponding to the edge with wk;min is
deﬁned as ak.

2.3. Phase 2: Path planning modeling
In multi-UAV area coverage missions, a "path- ﬁrst, sequence-
second" strategy using straight-line paths enhances mission ef ﬁ-
ciency and reduces redundancy. When a UAV's FOV intersects an
area's boundary, it creates path nodes that segment the area-
coverage ﬂight paths into three distinct parts, determined by the
different switching states of the UAVs' view payloads, as illustrated
in Fig. 4 . The study of DCCPP focuses on the node transfer re-
lationships between these three types of paths.
/C15 Operation Path: This ﬂight path is where the UAV's FOV effec-
tively covers the designated area, with coverage being active
only along this segment.
/C15 Entry Path: The path that transitions the UAV from its starting
position into the coverage area. This path con ﬁguration opti-
mizes the timing of UAV entry for mission execution.
/C15 Transfer Path: The ﬂight path used to switch between different
operation paths within the coverage area.

The model reveals that Ui's entry direction into area Zk
dynamically in ﬂuences the path length matrix Di;k. Therefore, the
problem of optimizing the node path sequence for the l k UAVs
covering Zk is formulated as a multiple dynamic traveling salesman
problem (MDTSP). In this context, Uk is considered as l k travelers,
with their initial positions representing the starting nodes of the
multiple travelers, and the operation paths corresponding to the
cities that must be visited. The state transition relationship from a
traveler's initial position to the ﬁrst city is analogous to the entry
path relationship, while the state transition from one city to
another corresponds to the transition path relationship. Addition-
ally, each city visit incurs a path cost, corresponding to the opera-
tion path relationship. Therefore, the DCCPP problem is de ﬁned as
optimizing the sequence of path node visits by multi-UAV starting
from their initial positions to achieve the shortest path length,
without returning to the starting point.
3. Solution approach
In the path planning process for multi-UAV coverage missions,
we assume each UAV is assigned to cover a single area, disregarding
cases where a UAV completes coverage of Zk and then transitions to
cover Zj. The construction algorithm for this process must meet the
following requirements:
/C15 Complete area coverage: The UAV's ﬂight paths must corre-
spond to a ﬁeld of view that covers the entire area.
/C15 Minimized overlap: The ﬁeld of view corresponding to the UAV's
ﬂight paths should avoid duplication or have the least amount of
duplication possible while ensuring the required overlap rate.
/C15 Optimized ﬂight efﬁciency: The total distance and ﬂight time of
the multi-UAV paths should be minimized.
/C15 Simplicity and ef ﬁciency: Simple operation strategies and al-
gorithms should be used to make the computation faster and
the planned paths easy to track and execute.
In order to meet the algorithm construction requirements out-
lined above, this section introduces the dynamic collaborative
coverage path planning algorithm. Initially, as discussed in Section
3.1, Dubins curves are applied to solve for three types of paths.
Subsequently, Section 3.2 optimizes the assignment of UAVs in each
area based on a greedy algorithm. Section 3.3 then introduces an
improved dynamic programming algorithm to optimize the three
types of path nodes within the area coverage. Finally, Section 3.4
utilizes an altitude descent algorithm to generate the mission's
3D path, constrained by the 2D planning path and the terrain's
altitude. The algorithm structure and process are illustrated in
Fig. 6 .

4.4.1. Method algorithm comparison and analysis
This section expands on the scenarios in Section 4.2 to evaluate
the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed algorithm in
solving the multi-UAV DCCPP problem. The count of the area to be
covered is extended to three, and U7 and U8 are randomly
distributed within the mission space to create a new scenario for
comparative analysis. During the path node optimization process,
the improved dynamic planning algorithm proposed in this paper
is compared with the TMSTC *, GA, and IACO.
TMSTC* uses a randomly generated tree with long straight
paths, applying an MSTC * strategy on a topological ring to balance
coverage time and turn cost, resulting in equal path costs for each
UAV. GA solves local optimal paths at each transition when
coverage is incomplete, generating each UAV's path iteratively.
IACO dynamically updates pheromone levels to balance path length
among UAVs, adding randomness to enhance global search capa-
bility. The parameters of IACO include a population size of 30,
pheromone importance of 1.5, heuristic importance of 5, global and
local pheromone decay rates of 0.3 and 0.01, respectively, and a
search factor of 0.3. The paths results generated by each algorithm
are shown in Fig. 16. Due to the random factors in the TMSTC * and
IACO, each algorithm was independently run 50 times for this
scenario. The comparison results of the multi-UAV path ef ﬁciency
are shown in Table 4.
The comparative results show that the IDP algorithm achieves
the shortest average path length across the three areas, high-
lighting its ability to effectively optimize various path types. This
capability allows UAVs to minimize turns and achieve compre-
hensive coverage more rapidly. The IDP algorithm accomplishes
this by decomposing the MDTSP problem into sub-problems
through a state transition equation, which evaluates path transi-
tion costs to ensure a globally optimal solution. In contrast, TMSTC *

4.4.2. Method various scenarios adaptability analysis
Through the various scenarios simulation experiments pre-
sented above, this section further analyzes the performance and
adaptability of the proposed method at different levels of
complexity.
Area Complexity: The size of the area, terrain complexity, and
relative coverage height directly in ﬂuence the dif ﬁculty of path
planning optimization. For example, the Z1 area in Section 4.2 is
mountainous, which has signi ﬁcant terrain ﬂuctuations. The area is
extensive and requires four UAVs for coverage. Therefore, the path
planning in this area must not only account for terrain variations
but also ensure that no area is left uncovered, increasing the
complexity of the optimization problem. In contrast, the Z2 area is
smaller, which has minimal terrain variations. Two UAVs are
assigned for coverage, resulting in lower computational complexity.
During the MDTSP problem-solving process, the algorithm's time
and space complexity is exponentially related to the modeled
operation path and the number of UAVs assigned. Hence, the
optimization model is established based on the proposed frame-
work, where UAVs are assigned according to allocation metrics,
reducing the complexity of solving the multi-area coverage prob-
lem. This approach is suitable for real-world distributed multi-UAV
cooperative mission optimization and execution.
Mission Complexity: During multi-UAV mission execution, the
triggering of dynamic events presents higher demands on the al-
gorithm's adaptability. For instance, when a UAV exits the mission
due to an unexpected situation, the system can re-plan the paths in
real-time and optimize the new area coverage path, demonstrating
the algorithm's capability to handle dynamic changes. Additionally,
the adaptability of the algorithm is further validated through both
coordinated and uncoordinated coverage of different areas. In the
case of multi-area coverage missions, the algorithm can effectively
schedule UAVs to ensure the mission is completed quickly and
efﬁciently.

5. Conclusions
In this paper, a solution algorithm for the DCCPP problem based
on an improved dynamic programming method is proposed. This
approach aims to address the optimization challenges posed by the
maneuvering constraints of UAVs during area coverage missions.
The main conclusions are summarized as follows:
(1) Firstly, under reasonable assumptions, this paper establishes
a multi-UAV DCCPP problem model, which includes several
key components: the UAV capability model, environmental
terrain model, complex area model, planning path model,
and area coverage mission model. By approximating and
convexifying the complex area, the area contour becomes
easier to plan. Additionally, to achieve ef ﬁcient complete
coverage path planning, the multi-UAV area coverage path is
classiﬁed into operation paths, entry paths, and transition
paths based on the UAV's FOV status.
(2) Then, this paper establishes the area allocation index and
employs the greedy algorithm for multi-UAV allocation

dimensional and altitude-plane hierarchical planning.
Based on the results of the two-dimensional path planning,
we utilize the altitude descent method to generate the three-
dimensional path for multi-UAV.
(3) Finally, this paper presents the design of multiple dynamic
scenarios and utilizes both digital simulation and mixed re-
ality semi-physical simulation to validate the effectiveness of
the proposed models and algorithms. The results demon-
strate that algorithms enhance path planning effectiveness
while meeting real-time requirements. This research holds
signiﬁcant implications for guiding the engineering appli-
cation of the proposed content, paving the way for its prac-
tical implementation.
In the future, we aim to enhance IDP optimization ef ﬁciency
through new improvement strategies and integration with
advanced algorithms to better address complex, real-time path
planning for multi-UAV missions like strike, relay, and assessment.
We'll also study emergency adjustment scenarios to improve
robustness and conduct large-scale simulations and real-world
tests.
